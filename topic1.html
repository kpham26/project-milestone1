Overview

Cyber threats include attempts to break into computer systems without permission, stealing information, corrupting or deleting data, or disrupting online services. Cyber threats affect people, organizations, schools, hospitals, and even governments. As our technology becomes more interconnected, cyber threats occur more frequently and become a bigger threat.

Some of the cyber attacks target the software and the network directly, while others take advantage of human mistakes. This is why cybersecurity is not only about the technical aspects but also about the human factor.

Common Types of Cyber Threats

  • Malware: Malicious software that aim to damage devices, steal information, or spy on users.
  • Ransomware: A type of malware that locks files or systems and demands payment to restore access.
  • Phishing: Fake emails, texts, websites or links that trick people into giving away their personal information.
  • DDoS: Distributed denial-of-service attacks that flood websites or services with heavy traffic so that they stop working.
  • SQL Injection: Attack that exploits weak database input fields to access or steal stored data.

Threat Comparison

Threat How It Works Main Impact
Malware Installs harmful software on a device Data theft, spying, or system damage
Ransomware Encrypts files and demands payment Loss of access and financial damage
Phishing Uses fake messages to deceive users Stolen passwords and identity theft
DDoS Overloads online services with traffic Downtime and service disruption
SQL Injection Injects malicious commands into a database Unauthorized access to sensitive data

Analysis

These threats show that cyber attacks are varied. Some attacks take advantage of technical vulnerabilities in systems, poor defenses, or vulnerable databases. Other attacks, such as phishing, rely on human factors by using trust, fear, or confusion.

This calls for multiple layers of protection for organizations as well as individuals. For effective cybersecurity, it is important to have the latest updates, use safe software, and have safe internet practices. Having a plan in case something goes wrong is also important.
